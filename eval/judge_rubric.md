## scope_accuracy
Does the letter describe exactly the approved services, correctly, and nothing else?
5 = every approved service is described correctly; nothing else is promised or suggested.
4 = all approved services are described correctly, with one minor vague phrase.
3 = one approved service is described loosely, or one small extra promise slips in.
2 = a service is described wrongly, or an unapproved service or outcome is promised.
1 = the scope is mostly wrong or misleading.

## clarity
Could the client understand what SB&P will do for them, and what happens next?
5 = completely clear; short sentences; no jargon left unexplained.
4 = clear, with one sentence that needs a second read.
3 = understandable, but generic or repetitive in places.
2 = hard to tell what will actually be done.
1 = confusing.

## tone
Is the tone right for an established audit and advisory firm (credible, plain, not flashy, no overpromising)?
5 = professional, plain and credible throughout.
4 = professional, with one phrase that is too salesy or too stiff.
3 = mostly professional, with several salesy, stiff, or casual phrases.
2 = the tone often undermines credibility (hype, guarantees, slang).
1 = inappropriate for a professional firm.

## completeness
Are the client's responsibilities and the timeline specific and useful for this engagement?
5 = responsibilities are specific to these services, and the timeline reflects the client's deadline.
4 = mostly specific, with one generic point.
3 = generic responsibilities or a vague timeline.
2 = responsibilities or timeline barely useful.
1 = missing or unusable.
